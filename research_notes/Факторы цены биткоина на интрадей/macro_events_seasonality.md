# Macro, event and calendar factors as short-horizon (1 h to a few days) drivers of BTC

Evidence-quality legend used below: **[A]** peer-reviewed / central-bank staff paper; **[B]** working paper, thesis, or research-firm note with stated method; **[C]** press/trader commentary or anecdote; **[Inf]** my inference (not sourced).

Time conversion (my arithmetic, not sourced; verify against a calendar). US releases at 08:30 ET = 12:30 UTC while the US is on daylight time (second Sunday of March to first Sunday of November) and 13:30 UTC otherwise. FOMC statement 14:00 ET = 18:00 / 19:00 UTC, press conference 14:30 ET = 18:30 / 19:30 UTC. NYSE/Nasdaq open 09:30 ET = 13:30 / 14:30 UTC, close 16:00 ET = 20:00 / 21:00 UTC. Riga is UTC+3 in summer and UTC+2 in winter, so when the US and EU are on the same clock regime: CPI/NFP/PCE = 15:30 Riga, FOMC statement = 21:00 Riga, press conference = 21:30 Riga, equity open = 16:30 Riga, equity close = 23:00 Riga. The regimes are out of sync for about 2-3 weeks each in March and October/November (today, 21 Oct 2026, the EU is on summer time until 25 Oct and the US until 1 Nov, so they are currently in sync). In those windows UTC times are fixed to US clocks while Riga offsets shift by one hour. Always build event timestamps in America/New_York, then convert.

## 1. How does BTC react to FOMC and CPI surprises? Initial move vs reversal within 1-24 h? Pre-announcement drift?

### Takeaway
Rigorous event studies find BTC's response to Fed-rate news is weak or insignificant, and the response to CPI surprises is large but unstable over time: strong in 2022-23, decayed in 2024-25, and re-strengthening at the 1-hour horizon in 2026. Reversal after the initial move is widely reported but only anecdotally; I found no systematic frequency study, and no crypto study of pre-FOMC drift.

### Cited Findings
- NY Fed Staff Report 1052 (Benigno and Rosa, Feb 2023) uses an intraday event-study design with ten sets of macro announcements (real economy, inflation, monetary policy; the monetary measures are Target, Path, LSAP). EUR/USD, gold and the S&P 500 react significantly to most news (2000-2022 sample). BTC (sample starts 2017) responses are "never significant at the 1 percent level", even for monetary policy news alone. Example: weak June 2016 payrolls moved the dollar about 1%, stocks about 0.5%, gold about 2%, while BTC went sideways; June 2021 FOMC moved the dollar, gold and stocks while BTC showed no systematic response. The authors flag that the short BTC sample leaves the question open. [A] — [Liberty Street Economics, Feb 2023](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2023/02/is-there-a-bitcoin-macro-disconnect/); paper: [NY Fed SR1052](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr1052.pdf)
- Secondary coverage of the same paper: BTC appears slightly sensitive to CPI (coefficient about 5%), and the CPI effect becomes insignificant when the sample is restricted to 2017-2021. I could not open the PDF text, so these details come from search-engine summaries of secondary coverage and are unverified against the paper. [B/C] — [Central Banking summary](https://www.centralbanking.com/node/7954481); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/bitcoin-doesnt-care-about-macro)
- Block Scholes (note by Thahbib Rahman; page shows July 2026 date, last updated 14 Sep 2026): regression of 1-hour BTC return on CPI surprise (headline YoY CPI minus median consensus), six years from 2020. Full sample: R² about 25%; a +1 pp upside surprise is associated with about -5.5% BTC over the next hour (0.1 pp is about -0.55%). Peak in Dec 2021-Nov 2023 (24-release rolling window): R² about 52%, beta about -13% per 1 pp. Decayed toward zero in late 2024-2025. June 2026 (24-release window): R² about 22%, beta -3.2%. On a 12-release window R² is nearly 80% in 2026, above the 2022-23 peak, but only at the 1-hour horizon, not at 4-24 h. After the June 2026 downside CPI surprise BTC rose toward $65K and closed up 2.9%. [B, small-sample rolling regressions, 12 observations per window] — [Block Scholes](https://www.blockscholes.com/institutional-research/is-bitcoin-showing-greater-sensitivity-to-us-cpi-releases-again)
- Same note, NFP: full-sample R² about 9% (a 100K payroll surprise about -0.16% over the hour); rolling R² peaked about 59% in June 2024, stayed above 30% into late 2024, and is near zero across horizons in 2026. The note does not report any reversal or mean-reversion analysis. [B] — [Block Scholes](https://www.blockscholes.com/institutional-research/is-bitcoin-showing-greater-sensitivity-to-us-cpi-releases-again)
- Context given in that note (as stated, not independently checked): US CPI went from 2.4% (Jan 2026) to above 4% (May), June printed 3.5% vs 3.8% consensus; markets moved from pricing two 2026 cuts to a possible hike by year end; the note refers to "Chair Warsh" testifying 14 July 2026. [B] — same source.
- K33 Research (Nov 2023, via CoinDesk): FOMC decisions had less medium-term directional impact on BTC as correlations moderated, but BTC still shows bursts of heightened, correlated volatility during FOMC hours; intraday swings were shrinking as the hiking cycle ended. [B/C] — [CoinDesk, 1 Nov 2023](https://coindesk-coindesk-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/markets/2023/11/01/federal-reserve-leaves-rates-unchanged-bitcoin-flat-at-345k/)
- K33 archive note (year not shown in the snippet I saw): an intraday correlation spike between BTC and US equity futures during an FOMC press conference, and an average minute-by-minute BTC move of about 0.8% in the press-conference hour. Date unverified. [B/C] — [K33 research](https://k33.com/research/archive/articles/bitcoin-outperforming-gold-and-equities-in-september-while-correlations) (the search result linked this, I did not open it; treat as unconfirmed)
- Thai journal study of FOMC meetings and crypto prices, 2021-2023: FOMC-linked volatility for BTC and ETH trended downward 2021-2023 (from search summary only, abstract not read). [B] — [J. of Social Communication](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JSC/article/view/271735)
- Jarboui and Mnif (2021): first COVID-era FOMC statements had a significant negative effect on most cryptocurrencies after 4 days (multi-day, not intraday; abstract-level only). [A/B] — [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JOIC-12-2020-0048/full/html)
- Reversal anecdotes: May 2022, BTC, Nasdaq 100 and S&P rose at the hike announcement and all reversed within 24 h [C] — [CoinDesk, May 2022](https://coindesk-coindesk-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/layer2/2022/05/08/the-fed-raised-rates-and-markets-freaked-out/). Jan 2024: BTC hit a day high of $38,930 then fell to $36,276 during the press conference (down 2.7% within the first hour of the statement) and recovered to about $36,800 by close [C] — [FXEmpire](https://amp.fxempire.com/en/bitcoin-btc-reacts-to-the-fed-policy-decision-and-press-conference/878925). Sept 2025 cut: BTC reportedly spiked to an intraday high of $116,318 before the statement, then reversed sharply [C] — trade press cited in my search; no primary link retained. CPI 2026: a report says BTC fell to $76,000 after a print and fully recovered within 30 min [C, mirror-site provenance, weak].
- A trader ("Astronomer") claims an "FOMC reversal model" with an over-85% hit rate and says a strong quarterly uptrend can weaken it; no published method, so unverified. [C] — [NewsBTC via TradingView](https://ru.tradingview.com/news/newsbtc%3A52494c660094b%3A0-bitcoin-price-forecast-key-scenarios-for-tomorrow-s-fomc-decision)
- Pre-announcement drift in equities: Lucca and Moench (NY Fed) found excess returns in the 24 h before scheduled FOMC announcements account for more than 80% of the equity premium over 17 years; it was not found for other asset classes or other announcements. Later work argues it may have disappeared in the last decade and is concentrated in high-uncertainty periods; one NBER paper reports continued drift for 15 days after. [A] — [Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/?p=1112); [NBER w24748](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24748/w24748.pdf); [Quantpedia on Martello and Ribeiro](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/?p=4197). I found **no** crypto-specific pre-FOMC drift study.

### Inferences
- [Inf] The two bodies of evidence are compatible: Fed-rate surprises (Target/Path) are hard to isolate from the statement-plus-press-conference two-stage release, and BTC's response is mostly volatility, not a stable signed effect; CPI surprise sign is a cleaner, regime-dependent predictor, mainly useful in the first hour.
- [Inf] Reported reversals are likely survivorship-biased (memorable days). A fair test needs a baseline: the share of random weekday hours at the same time of day whose first 30-60 min move is later reversed.
- [Inf] With about 8 FOMC meetings and 12 CPI releases per year, the effective sample since 2020 is only about 50 FOMC and about 75 CPI events. Rolling R² on 12 releases (as in Block Scholes) is extremely noisy; the "80%" figure should not be taken as a stable parameter.
- Testable hypotheses (Binance BTCUSDT 1-min or hourly bars; public CPI/FOMC dates; consensus forecasts need a free source such as the BLS release plus a consensus archive; if consensus is unavailable use the sign of the change in 2-year Treasury yield from FRED/Yahoo as a surprise proxy):
  - **H1 (CPI first-hour sign).** Condition: CPI release at 08:30 ET (12:30 or 13:30 UTC; 15:30 Riga), surprise in headline or core YoY or MoM at least 0.1 pp. Prediction: BTC return over [T, T+60 min] has the opposite sign to the surprise. Success: sign accuracy above 60% over 2023-2026 releases with binomial p below 0.05, and slope of return on surprise negative with Newey-West t below -2, evaluated separately for 2023, 2024-25 and 2026 to test the claimed decay and revival.
  - **H2 (post-CPI reversal).** Condition: |ret[T, T+15 min]| above 0.5%. Prediction: ret[T+15 min, T+4 h] has the opposite sign. Success: reversal share above 55% with a binomial test against the same statistic computed for the same clock minute on non-event weekdays (baseline), and positive net expectancy after 0.1% round-trip cost.
  - **H3 (FOMC two-stage reversal).** Condition: FOMC statement at 14:00 ET (18:00 or 19:00 UTC; 21:00 Riga). Define move1 = ret[T-1 min, T+30 min] (statement to pre-press-conference) and move2 = ret[T+30 min, T+4 h]. Prediction: sign(move2) is opposite to sign(move1) more than 50% of the time. Success: reversal rate above 60% on 2022-2026 meetings (about 35 events) and statistically above the same-hour non-FOMC-Wednesday baseline. Low power should be stated up front.
  - **H4 (pre-FOMC drift in BTC).** Condition: the 24 h ending at the statement time. Prediction: mean BTC return in this window exceeds the average 24 h return. Success: difference positive with bootstrap p below 0.1; expect low power (about 35-50 events), and treat a null result as informative given the weak equity-drift persistence.
  - **H5 (FOMC volatility).** Prediction: realized volatility in [T, T+90 min] on FOMC days exceeds the median of the same window on non-FOMC Wednesdays by at least 1.5x. Success: ratio above 1.5 with Wilcoxon p below 0.05; expected to be the most robust of the five.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full text of NY Fed SR1052 (PDF fetched as binary and no PDF renderer available), so its BTC-specific coefficients, event window and subsample splits are only from the Liberty Street post and secondary summaries.
- No systematic, peer-reviewed study of BTC reversal frequency within 1-24 h after FOMC or CPI; all reversal evidence is anecdotal.
- No crypto study of pre-FOMC drift; no study decomposing statement vs press conference for BTC.
- Block Scholes note could not be cross-checked; its Warsh/2026 macro narrative is taken as stated. Consensus-forecast data for CPI/NFP is not freely archived in a convenient form (not confirmed).
- PCE: no BTC-specific evidence found.

## 2. Intraday seasonality, weekend and day-of-week patterns; has it changed since spot ETFs (2024)?

### Takeaway
The robust finding is about volatility and volume, not direction: BTC variance has migrated heavily into US hours (about 13:00-22:00 UTC) and weekends are quieter, a shift that began around 2021 rather than with the 2024 ETFs. Directional hour-of-day and day-of-week return patterns are weak, unstable and mostly pre-2022.

### Cited Findings
- Kraken XBT/USD study (87,672 hourly observations, 2016-2025; MDPI *Journal of Risk and Financial Management* vol 19, issue 9, art. 707, as cited in secondary coverage): the nine hours 13:00-21:59 UTC (37.5% of the day) carried 50.6% of daily realized variance in 2022-2025 vs 38.4% in 2016-2018. The variance-weighted centre moved from 14.1 UTC to 17.1 UTC. The most volatile hour shifts from 14:00 UTC (US daylight time) to 15:00 UTC (standard time), tracking the NYSE open. On weekday NYSE holidays the US-hours variance share drops by 13.9 pp (55.7% to about 41.9%). Weekend/weekday volatility ratio: 0.96 (2016), 0.60 (2024), 0.64 (2025); volume ratio 0.78 (2016), 0.43 (2024), 0.46 (2025). Change-point analysis puts the main break at **November 2021**, with no local break at the Dec 2017 CME futures launch or Jan 2024 ETF approval; a broad before/after comparison suggests +9.6 pp for ETFs and +7.1 pp for CME, but a symmetric 12-month window shrinks these to 0.1 and 0.3 pp and a placebo test shows the broad method is significant for random dates too. The study does not attribute causation. Single exchange, ends 2025. [B/A; I only saw the CryptoSlate summary, MDPI page returned HTTP 403] — [CryptoSlate summary](https://cryptoslate.com/?p=561774); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/19/9/707)
- Kaiko (27 Jun 2024): after the 10-11 January 2024 ETF launches, volume at the 3-4 pm New York benchmark fixing window was over 6.7% of volume in Q1 2024 (4.5% in Q4 2023); on weekdays the US-close hour was over 6.6% of volume on benchmark exchanges vs just over 4% on weekends. Weekend volume share hit a 2024 low of 16% (28% peak in 2019). On weekdays in Q1, 20:00-21:00 UTC volume totalled over $17.6bn, the second-busiest hour behind 15:00 UTC. US share of market depth 45% vs 35% a year earlier. On Binance (not in ETF benchmark), hourly volume peaked at the US open and declined thereafter. [B] — [Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/btc-etfs-impact-on-spot-market-structure)
- Kaiko via Bloomberg/BNN (Mar-May 2024): volatility more concentrated in US opening hours; US-hours share of volume at a record 46% of cumulative 2024 volume through April; Thursday had the highest US-hours share (close to 15% of daily volume). [B/C, press coverage of Kaiko] — [Bloomberg, 21 May 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-05-21/share-of-bitcoin-volume-during-us-market-hours-surges-to-record); [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/news/bitcoin-volatility-is-more-pronounced-in-us-trading-hours-251428)
- Espel, SSRN 4779488 (2024): intraday regime seasonality in BTC and ETH around the US ETF introduction; patterns strongest on weekdays (abstract-level from search summary only; page returned 403). [B] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4779488)
- Intraday Dynamics of Bitcoin (5-minute data, 2019 paper, older sample): realized volatility highest at the opening hours of the three major stock markets. [A] — [Heriot-Watt portal](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/the-intraday-dynamics-of-bitcoin/)
- Risk Protocol study (via Blockworks): most volatile hours 02:00 and 15:00-18:00 UTC. [C/B] — [Blockworks](https://www.blockworks.com/news/bitcoins-price-predictable-study)
- Padyšák and Vojtko, "Seasonality, Trend-following, and Mean Reversion in Bitcoin" (SSRN 4081000), 2015-2021 Gemini data: hours 22:00 and 23:00 UTC show the largest and statistically significant positive returns (5% level); no hour is significantly negative; the weakest hours are 03:00-04:00 UTC (not significant). Rule: buy at 22:00 UTC, hold 2 h; reported annualized return about 33%, Sharpe 1.58, maximum drawdown -34%, no transaction costs, weak in bear markets. [B, pre-ETF, gross of costs] — [Quantpedia strategy page](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/intraday-seasonality-in-bitcoin)
- Day-of-week: Caporale and Plastun (2018) and Ma and Tanizaki (2019) find higher Monday returns; Baur, Cahill, Godfrey and Liu (15m observations, seven exchanges) find time-varying, non-persistent effects with only lower weekend volume and slightly higher Monday returns; Kinateder and Papavassiliou find no classical weekend effect but lower weekend risk and higher start-of-week volatility; NYDIG (2021) found outperformance on Fridays and early week fading later. [A/B, mixed] — [Quantpedia calendar-effects review](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-any-simple-calendar-effects-in-bitcoin-market/); [NYDIG](https://nydig.com/research/the-temporalities-of-bitcoin-returns)
- Velo session split (APAC 00-08 UTC, Europe 08-16, US 16-24) described as a short-window descriptive observation, not a study. [C] — [worldports.org mirror](https://www.worldports.org/?p=739589)

### Inferences
- [Inf] For a trader in Riga trading US hours: the tradable edge is concentrated in 15:30-23:00 Riga where variance is; direction cannot be assumed from time of day. 03:00-04:00 UTC (05:00-07:00 Riga) weakness and 22:00-23:00 UTC strength were pre-2022 effects and have not been shown to persist.
- [Inf] The 2021 break (not 2024) suggests the US-hours shift is a broader institutionalization (futures basis trading, market makers, Coinbase dominance, equity-linked flows), so ETFs "changed everything in 2024" is not supported by the one study that tested it with placebos.
- [Inf] Day-of-week return anomalies are probably not exploitable out of sample; day-of-week volatility (weekend about 0.6x weekday) is.
- Testable hypotheses:
  - **H6 (US-hours variance share).** Condition: Binance BTCUSDT 1-hour bars, 2022-2026. Prediction: share of daily realized variance in 13:00-22:00 UTC exceeds 48% in each of 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 YTD, and drops by at least 8 pp on NYSE holidays. Success: all four yearly shares above 48% and holiday drop confirmed with paired test p below 0.05.
  - **H7 (hour-of-day returns, out-of-sample).** Condition: long BTC 22:00-00:00 UTC (00:00-02:00 Riga winter; 01:00-03:00 summer) on all days. Prediction: positive mean return after costs in 2023-2026 (the original sample ended 2021). Success: mean above 0.03% net per trade and t-stat above 2, otherwise reject as decayed; apply a placebo of all 24 two-hour windows with Bonferroni correction.
  - **H8 (weekend).** Prediction: Saturday-Sunday realized volatility is 0.55-0.70 of weekday volatility in 2023-2026; Sunday 22:00 UTC to Monday 14:00 UTC mean return is not different from zero. Success: ratio inside the band each year; Monday-effect t-stat below 2.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the primary MDPI paper (HTTP 403) or SSRN Espel text; figures come via CryptoSlate and abstract summaries. The paper's author names were not shown.
- No post-2022 hour-of-day directional return study found; the 22-23 UTC anomaly has not been re-tested in sources I found.
- No Asia/Europe/US session return decomposition from a rigorous source (only Velo and Risk Protocol commentary).

## 3. US equity open and close effects; ETF creation/redemption timing

### Takeaway
BTC volatility peaks at the NYSE/Nasdaq open (14:00 UTC daylight, 15:00 UTC standard in the Kraken study) and volume surges around the 3-4 pm ET benchmark fixing window since the ETFs. I found no study showing a signed directional return effect at the open or close, and the exact ETF NAV timing is contested between sources.

### Cited Findings
- The Kraken study found the most volatile hour tracks the NYSE open and shifts with US daylight saving by one hour, with a variance-centre shift of 0.33 hours. [B] — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/?p=561774)
- Kaiko: volume at the fixing window 3-4 pm New York rose after ETF approval (6.7% of volume Q1 2024), averaging $220 million on Coinbase at the close; issuers base NAV on benchmark fixing prices over that window, so creations/redemptions require buying or selling BTC within the window. [B] — [Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/btc-etfs-impact-on-spot-market-structure)
- Conflicting NAV timing: a glossary entry claims NAV is struck at 4:00 pm London time using the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate (BRR), while CF Benchmarks describes a BRRNY synchronised to New York hours; unresolved — [Learn Bitcoin glossary](https://www.learnbitcoin.com/glossary/nav-net-asset-value); [CF Benchmarks](https://www.cfbenchmarks.com/blog/etc-groups-unique-tri-nav-etp-underpinned-by-cf-benchmarks-regulated-bitcoin-methodology). Kaiko says the Bitcoin New York Reference Rate observation window is one hour anchored at the 4 pm NY close.
- Kaiko also warns concentrated US-hours activity may raise US-hours volatility and may affect other hours. [B] — [Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/btc-etfs-impact-on-spot-market-structure)

### Inferences
- [Inf] Volume/volatility concentration at the open (13:30/14:30 UTC; 16:30 Riga) and in the fixing hour (19:00-20:00 UTC in US summer, 20:00-21:00 UTC in winter; about 22:00-23:00 Riga) is established; direction is not. ETF net-flow direction on the day could be linked to that day's return, but the flow data (published next morning by issuers) is a contemporaneous correlate, not a predictor.
- Testable hypotheses:
  - **H9 (open volatility).** Prediction: realized volatility of the 30 minutes after the US cash open exceeds the average 30-minute volatility of the day by at least 1.5x on at least 70% of non-holiday weekdays in 2024-2026. Success: confirmed; use America/New_York-aligned timestamps to avoid DST contamination.
  - **H10 (open continuation vs fade).** Condition: BTC return in the 30 minutes before the open (13:00-13:30 UTC daylight) and the first 30 minutes after it. Prediction: no sign relationship (null) or fade. Success metric: correlation of the two returns, with the claim "fade" accepted only if the correlation is below -0.1 with p below 0.05; otherwise treat the open as a volatility event only.
  - **H11 (fixing-window drift).** Prediction: ret over the 3-4 pm ET window correlates with same-day net ETF flows (positive), but the 3-4 pm return does not predict the next 4 h return. Success: contemporaneous correlation above 0.2, predictive correlation indistinguishable from zero.

### Gaps
- No study of the signed price effect of ETF creation/redemption timing; no clean source for which benchmark window each issuer uses; no hourly flow data.
- Yahoo hourly data is limited to 730 days; ETF intraday data for IBIT etc. would be needed for proper tests.

## 4. Nasdaq / S&P / DXY linkage at intraday horizons: lead-lag or contemporaneous?

### Takeaway
Documented correlations are contemporaneous and at daily frequency: BTC/Nasdaq correlation rose after institutional milestones, peaking at 0.87 in 2024 in one paper. I found no rigorous intraday lead-lag evidence for BTC vs Nasdaq futures or DXY, so the safe default is contemporaneous co-movement only.

### Cited Findings
- Di Wu (arXiv 2501.09911, Jan 2025): daily data from 2018, rolling correlation with Nasdaq-100 and S&P 500 rose after key institutional milestones (ETFs, corporate holdings) to a peak of 0.87 in 2024, with regime variation. Daily, not intraday. [B] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09911v1)
- K33: an FOMC press-conference-hour correlation spike between BTC and US equity futures moving in lockstep; year not confirmed. [B/C] — [K33](https://k33.com/research/archive/articles/bitcoin-outperforming-gold-and-equities-in-september-while-correlations)
- A Substack analysis finds that a one-day shift destroys the correlation, i.e. no daily lead-lag. [C] — [Ecoinometrics](https://ecoinometrics.substack.com/p/ecoinometrics-bitcoin-and-tech-stocks?s=r)
- NY Fed study: BTC does not respond like equities, FX or gold to macro news announcements (see section 1). [A]
- Kraken study: US-hours variance share falls on NYSE holidays, indicating dependence of BTC activity on equity-market hours. [B] — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/?p=561774)

### Inferences
- [Inf] Equity-hours correlation is probably conditional (high at US open and during macro events, lower overnight and weekends). Correlation is not predictive: predicting BTC from a concurrent NQ move needs a lead that sources do not document.
- [Inf] DXY: no sourced evidence; the negative BTC-DXY relation is commonly asserted but I did not verify it at intraday frequency.
- Testable hypotheses:
  - **H12 (lead-lag).** Data: Yahoo NQ=F or ES=F hourly (last 730 days) vs Binance BTCUSDT 1-hour returns, US session only. Prediction: the cross-correlation of NQ return(t) with BTC return(t+1h) is not significantly different from zero, while lag-0 correlation is above 0.3. Success: lag-1 correlation |r| below 0.05 on both sides (that is, no exploitable lead), otherwise report the lead and test on a hold-out half.
  - **H13 (regime).** Prediction: 30-day rolling correlation of hourly returns between BTC and NQ is greater during 14:30-21:00 UTC than 00:00-13:00 UTC. Success: the difference is positive in at least 80% of rolling windows in 2024-2026.
  - **H14 (DXY).** Condition: DXY hourly (DX-Y.NYB) moves above 0.2% in an hour. Prediction: contemporaneous BTC return has the opposite sign more than 60% of the time but next-hour BTC return does not. Success: contemporaneous sign agreement above 60%, next-hour agreement within 50 plus or minus 5%.

### Gaps
- No academic or research-firm study on intraday lead-lag between BTC and Nasdaq/S&P futures; none on DXY and BTC at intraday horizons. The search found only daily or 2018-2019-era papers.

## 5. CME gaps: fill statistics, random baseline, and the 24/7 change

### Takeaway
The trader's belief is largely right for the future: CME made its crypto futures and options trade 24/7 starting 4:00 pm CT on Friday 29 May 2026, which removes the Friday-close to Sunday-open weekend gap (only a short weekend maintenance window remains). For history, the only fill-rate figures I found are anecdotal (about 79 of 80; 8 of 10), none against a random-level baseline, so "gaps fill" is unproven rather than established.

### Cited Findings
- CME Group (press release 1 Jun 2026 as reported): 24/7 trading of crypto futures and options went live at 4:00 pm Central on Friday 29 May 2026 on CME Globex; products reported include futures and options on Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, XRP, Cardano, Chainlink, Stellar, Avalanche, Sui, plus Micro Bitcoin and Micro Ether; Bitcoin Volatility futures also 24/7. First weekend: more than 7,200 contracts, about $50 million notional. Weekend trades carry the next business day's trade date. ### Inferences
- [Inf] High fill rates are expected mechanically: a price following a random walk with BTC's volatility revisits a nearby level (the gap is typically a few percent or less) with high probability within days or weeks, whether or not "magnetism" exists. Without a random-level baseline (same-size "pseudo-gaps" placed at arbitrary times with no market closure), fill statistics prove nothing. Time-to-fill and maximum adverse excursion before fill matter far more than the fill rate.
- [Inf] Since 29 May 2026 weekend-closure gaps should be gone or tiny. The remaining small gap risk comes from the weekend maintenance window (about 2 h) and from CME's daily settlement/trade-date conventions, but all testing of that needs post-launch data (about 5 months to Oct 2026 = about 20 weekends, too few for statistics). Gaps in older CME data remain a historical curiosity for backtests, not a live signal.
- [Inf] The trader's claim "gaps are no longer relevant" is supported in structure from 29 May 2026; his claim as applied to pre-2026 history is untested here.
- Testable hypotheses:
  - **H15 (historical gap fill vs placebo).** Data: Yahoo BTC=F daily (CME front month, 2018-May 2026) for the Friday close and Sunday-evening/Monday open gap; Binance BTCUSDT 1-hour for fill detection (price touches Friday-close level). Prediction: P(fill within 7 days) exceeds a placebo, where placebo gaps are constructed by taking a random weekday hour's close and the price 48 h later (same elapsed time, no market closure), restricted to the same absolute gap size. Success: real-gap fill rate exceeds placebo by at least 10 pp with a bootstrap p below 0.05 and median time-to-fill shorter. Report also the median maximum adverse excursion before fill.
  - **H16 (trading the fill).** Condition: gap above 1% (as per CME), entry at Sunday open (23:00 UTC with CME on US daylight time / Sunday 22:00 UTC; Riga about 00:00 Monday or 01:00), target = Friday close, stop = 1x gap size beyond entry. Success: net expectancy per trade above 0 after 0.1% round-trip costs, hit rate above the stop-adjusted break-even, on an out-of-sample 2023-May 2026 set; expected to fail if fill rate is not above placebo.
  - **H17 (post-launch).** Condition: weekends from 29 May 2026 on. Prediction: CME front-month Friday-close to Sunday-open absolute gap, as a percentage, is smaller than the pre-launch median by at least 70%. Success: median post-launch gap below 0.3x pre-launch median; N is small (about 20 weekends), so report the exact counts rather than significance.
